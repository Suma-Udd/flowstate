Verified deviations from the design notes

  1. --accent-soft (dark) is #1A3330, not #1E3A37. Active nav is --accent text on --accent-soft. At the spec value that pair measured 4.19:1, under the 4.5:1 AA floor for 13px --t-label nav items. The new value measures 4.60:1 and is visually near-identical.
  2. --text-dim (light) is #68655F, not #6B6862. Dim metadata inside a hovered row is --text-dim on --surface-hover, the darkest light surface. At the spec value that pair measured 4.45:1 — 0.05 under the floor, and invisible to the eye, which is exactly why the spec says check rather than eyeball. The new value measures 4.66:1 there and lifts every other --text-dim pair with it.
  3. --text-faint is disabled-only. The spec lists it for “placeholders, disabled”, but it measures 2.95:1 (light) and 3.17:1 (dark) on --surface. Disabled controls are exempt from WCAG 1.4.3; placeholder text is not. base.css therefore renders ::placeholder in --text-dim. Raising --text-faint to pass would collapse it into --text-dim and destroy the three-tier text hierarchy.
  4. --border-strong is a decorative hairline, not a component boundary. It measures ~1.6:1 and does not meet WCAG 1.4.11’s 3:1. Inputs are identified by their --surface-alt fill and their label rather than by the hairline alone, and the focus indicator carries the non-text contrast obligation at 7.95:1 (light) / 5.84:1 (dark). Darkening the hairline to 3:1 reads as a heavy box and breaks the papery aesthetic that is the whole point of the palette.
  5. Data ramp steps sit 1.4–1.8:1 apart. That is inherent to a four-step sequential ramp and is not a WCAG failure — adjacent steps are fills, not text on each other. --ramp-3 and --ramp-4 both clear 3:1 against --surface, so a single bar is always distinguishable from its background.
